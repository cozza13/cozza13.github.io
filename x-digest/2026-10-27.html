Glioma research roundup: an MIT ultrasound drug implant, CD70 CAR-T in mice, and blood and spinal-fluid DNA tests

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • MIT built a flexible implant (LUCID) as thin as cardstock that releases drugs, then uses 24 tiny ultrasound transducers to push them deeper into tissue. It is aimed at cancer cells left behind after glioblastoma surgery and has only been tested in mice.
  • CD70 CAR-T therapy in mice raised the number of the body's own CD8 T cells in glioblastoma and lowered Tregs and M2-like macrophages; the poster calls it 'a compelling single-cell finding—not clinical efficacy.'
  • Nature Cancer's October issue includes a paper titled 'Stroke fuels glioma progression' (headline only in the post).
  • A Mayo Clinic study looks at testing DNA in cerebrospinal fluid when a brain tumor diagnosis is uncertain (headline only).
  • Prima Mente's Pleiades-Neuron model detects DNA released by dying neurons in blood down to a part-per-million level.

Need to know: The theme is getting drugs further into the brain and reading tumor or brain signals without surgery. All of it is early: mouse studies, headlines and new methods, not patient results.

A careful look at a viral claim that two antiparasitic drugs kept a glioblastoma stable

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • The post discusses a reported case: a 41-year-old with IDH-wildtype glioblastoma had surgery, radiation and temozolomide, then added ivermectin and mebendazole, and scans were described as stable six months later.
  • The author points out that 'stable' is not the same as the tumor shrinking or disappearing, and that scans after chemoradiation are hard to read because treatment changes can look like tumor.
  • Key information is missing: how much tumor was left after surgery, earlier MRIs, maintenance temozolomide, MGMT methylation status, and whether standard response criteria were used.
  • The author says stability after standard treatment cannot automatically be credited to drugs added later. The post is cut off while discussing mebendazole research.

Need to know: Repurposed-drug stories spread fast in the GBM community. This is a useful checklist for judging any single-case report.

Obesity and a larger waist linked to cancer in people in their 20s and 30s, in a 6.3-million-person study

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • The Annals of Internal Medicine study followed 6.3 million South Koreans aged 20 to 39 with no prior cancer for 11 years, by BMI and waist size. Topol's post does not give the result numbers.
  • Ben Bikman says waist size matters because belly fat goes along with insulin resistance, and the high insulin that comes with it is a growth signal (his post is cut off).
  • Another commenter notes that Korean studies use Asia-Pacific cutoffs, where obesity starts at BMI 25, and that in the same database a BMI of 30 or more nearly doubled young-onset pancreatic cancer risk (HR 1.96; median age at diagnosis 41).
  • Bikman also cites the Women's Health Initiative: postmenopausal women not on hormone therapy with the highest fasting insulin had 2.4 times the breast cancer risk of those with the lowest, even after accounting for estradiol.

Need to know: This is large observational data tying belly fat and high insulin to cancer risk. It fits the metabolic view of cancer, but it shows a link, not cause and effect.

Bikman on ketosis: a ketone drink blunted a glucose spike, and ketosis is the body's default when insulin falls

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • When healthy young adults drank a ketone supplement 30 minutes before a standard glucose drink, their blood sugar rose less and they released less insulin, which points to better insulin sensitivity (the post is cut off).

Carbs and appetite: fast carbs drove 81% more eating, and fructose let gut toxins into the blood within three days

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • When obese teenage boys ate same-calorie meals with different glycemic index, they ate 81% more over the next five hours after the high-glycemic meals.
  • Fast carbs drove insulin up and glucagon down, and then glucose and fatty acids fell and epinephrine rose, leaving them hungry despite plenty of stored fuel.
  • Swapping some starch for fructose for three days at the same calories raised blood endotoxin and a liver enzyme in healthy young adults; the same amount of glucose did not.
  • In type 2 diabetes, eating protein and vegetables 10 minutes before carbs cut the glucose rise by about half, which Bikman calls a workaround compared with cutting the carbs.

Need to know: Carb quality affects hunger and inflammation beyond calories. These studies help explain why keto often makes appetite easier to manage.
