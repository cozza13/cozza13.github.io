For you 6 | Unique 6

King's College London review: vorasidenib in grade 2 non-enhancing IDH-mutant glioma

For you 5 | Unique 5

  • @DrMofatteh and Prof Keyoumars Ashkan (King's Neuro) posted their systematic review of vorasidenib for IDH-mutant diffuse glioma.
  • It covers 10 studies with 488 patients. In INDIGO, vorasidenib gave longer PFS than placebo (27.7 vs 11.1 months; HR 0.39) and delayed the next anticancer treatment (HR 0.26).
  • Adverse events were mostly grade 1–2. Transaminase elevations were reversible.
  • The authors say long-term survival, patient selection, sequencing and neurocognitive outcomes are still open questions.

Need to know: This covers IDH-mutant low-grade glioma, not IDH-wildtype GBM. It shows how targeted therapy is changing the IDH-mutant side of neuro-oncology. Evidence: Established for PFS (phase 3 INDIGO); the review itself is a preprint on Research Square and SSRN.

Other cancer

Nature Immunology: activating non-tumor-specific "bystander" T cells inside tumors was enough to eliminate them in mice

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • Ghirardelli Smith et al. (University of Minnesota and the Broad Institute; published Sept 8 and resurfacing this week) activated virus-specific memory CD8 T cells inside B16 melanoma tumors that don't carry the viral antigen.
  • Tumors were still cleared when tumor cells couldn't present the antigen at all (H-2Kb knockout), and when T cells lacked perforin.
  • Killing ran through paracrine signals: IFN-γ and TNF from T cells plus nitric oxide from recruited myeloid cells, causing caspase-dependent death. Blocking IFN-γ and TNF together prevented clearance.
  • Gene signatures of this response predicted survival in human melanoma patients.
  • @youngkwangchae called it one of the most impactful tumor immunology papers of the year. @niklas_kluemper flagged it as relevant to cold tumors and antigen-loss escape, and noted that clinical translation isn't established.

Need to know: If local T-cell activation can work without tumor-specific recognition, that matters for tumors with few or variable antigens, which is a known problem in GBM. All of the data is preclinical (mouse plus human cell lines). Evidence: Emerging.

@youngkwangchae · Nature Immunology · @niklas_kluemper

Nature Medicine: AI-scored TROP2 membrane ratio predicts who benefits from datopotamab deruxtecan in lung cancer

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • The paper, flagged by @youngkwangchae, describes a fully supervised computational-pathology model (TROP2 QCS) that measures TROP2 in each cell's membrane and cytoplasm on standard IHC slides.
  • The normalized membrane ratio (NMR) was tuned in TROPION-PanTumor01 (n = 115) and validated in TROPION-Lung01 (n = 221, nonsquamous, no actionable alterations).
  • Among Dato-DXd patients, median PFS was 7.2 vs 4.0 months for NMR-positive vs NMR-negative tumors (HR 0.40). Docetaxel patients showed no difference (interaction P = 0.0098).
  • Conventional pathologist TROP2 scoring didn't predict outcomes. The biomarker is now being tested prospectively as a companion diagnostic in phase 3 TROPION-Lung17.

Need to know: It's a concrete case of AI pathology finding a predictive signal that human scoring missed. That's the kind of tool that could eventually help choose ADCs in other cancers. Evidence: Emerging (retrospective validation; prospective trial underway).

@youngkwangchae · Nature Medicine

FDA oncology approvals this week: bezuclastinib + sunitinib for GIST, and giredestrant for adjuvant ER+ breast cancer

For you 6 | Unique 5

  • Nov 24: the FDA approved bezuclastinib (Ekitna) plus sunitinib for advanced GIST after imatinib, based on the phase 3 PEAK trial (n = 413), which Dana-Farber led.
  • In PEAK, median PFS was 16.5 vs 9.2 months with sunitinib alone (HR 0.50), and ORR was 46% vs 26%. OS was immature, and the label carries a boxed warning for hepatotoxicity.
  • Nov 23: the FDA approved giredestrant (Wilserdly) as adjuvant therapy for intermediate- to high-risk ER+/HER2− stage I–III breast cancer. The lidERA results were published in NEJM on Nov 25.
  • In lidERA (n = 4,170), invasive disease-free survival favored giredestrant (HR 0.70; 3-year 92.4% vs 89.6%). The NSABP calls it the first new endocrine therapy in more than 20 years to beat standard of care in this setting.

Need to know: Two big targeted-therapy approvals landed during a holiday week. Neither involves brain tumors. Bezuclastinib went through FDA's expedited programs (priority review, breakthrough designation, Real-Time Oncology Review). Evidence: Established (phase 3).

2. AI updates

A Stanford physicist's quantum-gravity proposal, built with GPT-6 Astra, dominates the feed, and a leading physicist pushes back

For you 6 | Unique 7

  • Venkatesa "Ven" Chandrasekaran (Stanford; widely described on X as an OpenAI researcher) posted a single-author arXiv preprint proposing a background-independent quantization of 4D vacuum gravity built on null cones.
  • Its acknowledgements say several calculations and proofs came out of months of back-and-forth with GPT-6 Astra, using about 400 million tokens.
  • @sama praised him as an early believer that current models could take on Millennium Prize problems (about 590K views). @MTSlive's post about it reached about 64K views.
  • Physicist Jonathan Oppenheim wrote that it's a serious paper that "doesn't even come close to establishing a quantum theory of gravity." He had a swarm of Claude referees list 21 known obstacles; by their count the paper solves 1, sidesteps 7, assumes away 6 and doesn't mention 7.
  • @minilek argues that releasing AI-generated research math shows the world how fast capability is moving.

Need to know: Frontier models are now credited as collaborators on top-tier theory papers, but credit isn't validation. The useful pattern for health research is the same one: AI helps you move fast, and independent experts still have to referee the result. Evidence: Speculation (unreviewed preprint).

Mixture-of-Loops Transformer: looped-model depth without the extra inference latency

For you 5 | Unique 6

  • @BoWang87 introduced MoLT (led by Lianghui Zhu with @xinggangwang). Loops pass keys and values forward as reusable memory, so later loops don't recompute them.
  • It reports 1.7× pretraining token efficiency at 700M parameters and consistent gains over OLMo3 at 700M, 1.5B dense and 3B MoE.
  • Inference latency was on par with non-looped Transformers at small scale. Continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base gave 1.4× token efficiency.
  • Paper and code are public. The post got about 19K views and 207 bookmarks.

Need to know: It's an architecture-efficiency result rather than a product. If it holds up at scale, small open models could get cheaper to run. Evidence: Emerging (preprint, small scales).

@BoWang87

3. Keto updates

Open models got better at estimating a plate's carbs after seeing one example with real weights

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • @MaziyarPanahi (creator of @OpenMed_AI) showed an open model one plate with its real gram weights, then asked for carb estimates on 506 more plates.
  • Its average miss fell from 10.2 g to 7.8 g of carbs per plate.
  • He says the other open models he tried improved too.
  • He posted it on Thanksgiving "for everyone counting carbs today."

Need to know: A 7.8 g average miss is still large against a tight daily carb budget, so photo-based carb estimates aren't precise enough to log by. Anchoring the model with one weighed example is an easy trick to try in a meal-logging pipeline. Evidence: Anecdotal (single informal benchmark).

@MaziyarPanahi
