FDA opened its Rare Disease Framework Initiative, with public comments open until Dec 11

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • FDA held a hybrid public meeting on Oct 28 with the Reagan-Udall Foundation to launch a Rare Disease Framework Initiative.
  • The post says only 5% of rare diseases have approved treatments, and FDA wants input on better ways to review therapies for small populations like GBM.
  • The comment docket is FDA-2026-N-12214 on regulations.gov and stays open until Dec 11.
  • DCVax-L supporters are submitting comments asking for clearer guidance on when external control arms are acceptable, since the DCVax-L phase 3 used them.
  • Advocates are also pushing to change Right to Try so patients don't have to exhaust standard of care first.

Need to know: This is the most concrete policy opening for GBM therapies in a while. Anyone can file a comment before Dec 11, and the DCVax-L community is treating it as a pathway question, separate from the still-pending UK decision.

ESMO 2026 lung cancer wrap: SANOVO, OptiTROP-Lung04 and NeoCOAST-2

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • SANOVO: savolitinib plus osimertinib up front in EGFR-mutant, MET-overexpressing lung cancer gave median PFS of 24.9 vs 13.8 months overall (HR 0.56); overall survival looks promising but is immature.
  • OptiTROP-Lung04: the antibody-drug conjugate sac-TMT beat platinum chemo after EGFR-drug failure, with final OS of 25.9 vs 17.4 months (HR 0.69).
  • NeoCOAST-2, a lung cancer study, was published in Nature alongside its ESMO presentation.
  • Pfizer's talazoparib plus enzalutamide was reported as FDA approved on Oct 26 for HRR-mutated prostate cancer.

Need to know: Antibody-drug conjugates and targeted-drug combinations keep moving earlier in treatment, which is the main ESMO trend this year.

Keto updates

Glucose swings hurt blood vessels more than steadily high glucose

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • When glucose was pushed back and forth between normal and high over a day, arteries relaxed less well and oxidative stress rose more than when glucose stayed steadily high.
  • That was true in healthy people and in people with type 2 diabetes.
  • Bikman notes an A1C only shows the average, not the swings, and suggests building meals around protein and fat.

Need to know: Steady glucose matters, not just low glucose, which is one more reason a CGM tells you more than an A1C.

Type 2 diabetes at a normal BMI went into remission with 6 to 7% weight loss

For you 7 | Unique 6

Need to know: Liver fat and insulin tell you more than BMI does.
