Need to know: No major GBM news today, but drug delivery across the blood-brain barrier keeps coming up. The ultrasound safety data look good, but survival benefit is still unproven, and BNCT phase 3 results will be worth watching.

Brain metastases at ESMO: T-DXd, tucatinib, and intrathecal HER2 antibodies

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • DESTINY-Breast09 exploratory analysis: in 61 patients with stable brain metastases, T-DXd plus pertuzumab cut the risk of brain progression (CNS-PFS HR 0.26) vs the standard THP, and CNS response was 51.6% vs 26.7%.
  • BRIDGET (phase 2): adding tucatinib after local therapy for brain-only progression in HER2+ breast cancer gave durable intracranial control; randomized data are still needed.
  • INSPIRE-LM (phase 2): radiation followed by intrathecal trastuzumab/pertuzumab for HER2+ leptomeningeal metastasis.

Need to know: These are breast-cancer brain metastases, not primary GBM. Still, they show some drugs reaching and working in the brain, and drugs delivered into spinal fluid, which matters for neuro-oncology in general.

Other cancer

Oxaloacetate, a normal energy-metabolism compound, linked to clearer thinking in breast cancer survivors with 'chemo brain'

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • A UCLA study in JCO Oncology Advances gave breast cancer survivors with serious memory and thinking problems oral oxaloacetate twice a day for eight weeks.
  • Every woman who finished reported sharper thinking, and the group's average scores moved from clearly impaired into the normal range.
  • Participants also reported less fatigue; side effects were mostly mild stomach issues.
  • There was no comparison group, so the authors say larger studies are needed.

Need to know: A metabolic, cheap approach to cancer-related brain fog. It's a small, uncontrolled study, so treat it as a lead, not proof.

ESMO: personalized cancer vaccines are moving from 'does it trigger immunity' to 'does it last'

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Patrick Ott reviewed a decade of neoantigen vaccines: in INTerpath-001, intismeran plus pembrolizumab improved recurrence-free survival in resected melanoma (HR 0.58).
  • He noted that vaccine-induced T cells still recognize only a fraction of the selected neoantigens.
  • Juliane Walz said the adjuvant/minimal-residual-disease setting may give vaccines their best chance.
  • In CLL, a personalized peptide vaccine triggered T-cell responses in 95% of patients and reduced MRD in 90%.

Need to know: Experts agree vaccines can trigger an immune response; the question now is lasting clinical benefit. That's the same question hanging over dendritic-cell vaccines like DCVax-L.

MOIO phase 3: giving immunotherapy only every 3 months to responders looked worse for survival

For you 6 | Unique 7

  • French academic trial (n=340): patients responding after 6 months of immunotherapy got either standard dosing or a dose every 3 months.
  • PFS HR 1.35 (median 21.9 vs 25.0 months); the futility boundary was crossed.
  • OS HR 2.0 (1.1 to 3.9) with the reduced schedule.
  • Severe immune side effects were similar (9.5% vs 10.5%), so the reduced schedule didn't make treatment safer.

Need to know: A rare randomized test of cutting immunotherapy back, and it came out against blanket de-escalation.

ESMO: sacituzumab tirumotecan nearly triples PFS in PD-L1-negative lung cancer and extends survival in triple-negative breast cancer

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • OptiTROP-Lung06 (phase 3, NEJM): sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab vs chemo plus pembrolizumab in PD-L1-negative nonsquamous lung cancer, median PFS 15.0 vs 5.7 months (HR 0.49), with fewer grade 3+ side effects; OS is still immature.
  • OptiTROP-Breast01 final analysis: in pretreated metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, OS 14.7 vs 9.9 months vs chemo (HR 0.60).
  • Separately, the SWITCH trial found that a second ADC barely worked after a first one when only the target changed (ORR 0 to 8.3%); changing the payload seemed to matter.

Need to know: Sac-TMT is a TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate, and here it beat chemo in a first-line setting. The SWITCH result suggests resistance is about the drug payload, not the target.

Keto updates

Ben Bikman: one short night of sleep makes healthy people insulin resistant the next day

For you 8 | Unique 6

  • After one night of about four hours' sleep, healthy adults' insulin worked roughly 25% less well, in both liver and muscle.
  • Bikman says many people then lean on a carb-heavy breakfast to get through the morning.
  • His advice: make sleep a priority, and keep carbs especially low the day after a bad night.
  • Separately, a Brain-list physician notes one bad night raises the hunger hormone ghrelin by 28%.

Need to know: This links sleep and ketosis directly: for anyone tracking GKI, a short night can push glucose and insulin up the next day, so keeping carbs extra low on those days matters more.

High fasting insulin linked to about 5x Alzheimer's rates, and early-life sugar rationing to lower dementia risk

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • In older adults without diabetes and without APOE4, those with high fasting insulin had Alzheimer's at about five times the rate of those with normal insulin.
  • Bikman notes the study looked at a single point in time, so it can't prove cause, and suggests asking for fasting insulin along with glucose.
  • A UK Biobank analysis found people exposed to Britain's sugar rationing in the womb and early childhood had a 27% lower dementia risk and slightly slower brain aging decades later (the post text is cut off after the 27%).

Need to know: Two different studies point the same way: chronically high insulin and sugar exposure are bad for the brain.
