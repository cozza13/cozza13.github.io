ESMO 2026: full INTerpath-001 data for Moderna and Merck's personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • Phase 3, double-blind, 1,137 patients with resected stage IIB to IV melanoma: intismeran plus pembrolizumab vs placebo plus pembrolizumab, median follow-up 24.6 months.
  • 24-month recurrence-free survival was 82.2% vs 70.0% (HR 0.58); 24-month distant-metastasis-free survival was 88.5% vs 80.2% (HR 0.62).
  • Grade 3 or worse side effects were 23.6% vs 20.3%; fever was 52.2% vs 8.2%.
  • An analyst notes no difference in the first six months (HR 0.99), immature overall survival, about six weeks to make each patient's dose, and no FDA approval yet; Moderna is aiming for a possible 2027 launch.

Need to know: This is the full Presidential Symposium presentation after yesterday's early release. Personalized neoantigen vaccines are the closest relative of the dendritic-cell and vaccine ideas discussed in GBM, but this result is in melanoma only.

ESMO 2026: tarlatamab added to maintenance extends survival in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (DeLLphi-305)

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • Phase 3 DeLLphi-305 (563 patients) added tarlatamab, a T-cell engager, to durvalumab maintenance after first-line chemo-immunotherapy.
  • Overall survival was 21.1 vs 12.7 months (HR 0.42); progression-free survival was 7.4 vs 1.9 months (HR 0.34).
  • A steering-committee member posted cytokine release syndrome at 49.6%, grade 3 or worse 0.4%.
  • Posters called it practice-changing; it was presented in the first Presidential Symposium.

Need to know: An HR of 0.42 for overall survival is unusually large for a phase 3 trial. It's a sign that T-cell engagers are working in a hard-to-treat, fast-growing cancer.

Bikman: insulin resistance is a growth problem too, after a UK Biobank study links insulin sensitivity to fewer GI cancers

For you 8 | Unique 6

  • A UK Biobank study found that better insulin sensitivity (measured as eGDR) is linked to a lower risk of gastrointestinal cancers.
  • Bikman: when cells stop responding to insulin the pancreas makes more of it, and insulin is 'a powerful growth signal that many tumor cells are happy to use.'

Need to know: It ties directly into the metabolic view of cancer: high insulin as a growth signal is part of why low-insulin, low-glucose approaches are studied. Research context only.

Science: a wireless implant removes blood glucose electrically and raised ketones in diabetic mice

For you 6 | Unique 8

  • The ECHO implant (Maity, Fussenegger and colleagues, Science, published Oct 22) converts circulating glucose into gluconate, which is mostly cleared by the kidneys.
  • In obese diabetic db/db mice it normalized glucose within about 60 to 90 minutes and kept control for six weeks without detectable hypoglycemia.
  • Treated mice lost up to 10% of body weight with unchanged food intake, with lower insulin and triglycerides and higher beta-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate; blood pH stayed stable.
  • Linked to a CGM, it switched on and off by itself over 12 days; it's a preclinical proof of concept only.

Need to know: An unusual way to lower glucose and raise ketones directly, without drugs or diet. It's mouse-only, but it shows interest in controlling the glucose supply itself.

Bikman: insulin resistance shows up in the brain, in lean young women with PCOS

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • Normal-weight women in their 20s with PCOS, matched by BMI, had 10% higher fasting glucose and 40% higher insulin resistance.
  • Their brains used 9 to 14% less glucose in parts of the frontal, parietal and temporal cortex.
  • The more insulin resistant a woman was, the less glucose her hippocampus and amygdala used; memory tests were still normal.
  • Bikman notes it is cross-sectional, so it shows association, not cause.

Need to know: More data that insulin resistance affects how the brain uses fuel, even at normal weight. Fasting insulin is a useful marker to watch alongside glucose.
