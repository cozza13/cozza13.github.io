Glioblastoma

UCLA GBM vaccine data: giving PD-1 blockade too early may let suppressive macrophages get there first

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • The post summarizes a UCLA abstract (#819) on the ATL-DC dendritic-cell vaccine with poly-ICLC plus PD-1 blockade in recurrent GBM.
  • When PD-1 blockade started before and after surgery alongside the vaccine (Arm A), the pathway that pulls in immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages switched on before vaccine-primed T cells arrived.
  • Median overall survival was 271 days in Arm A versus 555 days when the vaccine came first and PD-1 blockade followed (Arm B), per the post.
  • The abstract's conclusion names macrophages as a major obstacle and points to targeting them (for example CSF1R blockade) as a next step.
  • The poster is a $NWBO investor account; a news write-up of the same UCLA dual-immunotherapy work was shared by @peter_brit.

Need to know: This adds a concrete mechanism and timing detail to yesterday's UCLA immunotherapy item. Treat the survival split as small-arm abstract data, not a head-to-head result.

FDA grants accelerated approval to dostarlimab for untreated stage II/III dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancer

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • GSK's Jemperli (dostarlimab, a PD-1 inhibitor) is now approved for previously untreated locally advanced dMMR/MSI-H rectal cancer, the first immunotherapy in this setting.
  • In the phase 2 AZUR-1 trial, 110 of 154 patients (71%) needed no surgery, radiation or chemo and had no progression at data cutoff (@ozdogan_md).
  • The investigator-assessed 12-month clinical complete response rate was 68% (@ErulEnes); the approval came about four months early (@pharmdaz).
  • Dosing is up to 9 doses every 3 weeks over about 6 months; it's an accelerated approval, so durable benefit still has to be confirmed (@DrAllanPereira).
  • Full data are due at ESMO on Sunday, Oct 25.

Need to know: The biggest oncology regulatory move of the window, and a clear example of a biomarker (dMMR/MSI-H) letting some patients skip surgery and chemoradiation entirely.

Keto updates

Bikman: ketone (BHB) infusion cut muscle protein loss by more than 70% during inflammation

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • Healthy men were given a bacterial toxin to cause short-term inflammation, then infused with BHB.
  • With BHB in the blood, forearm muscle protein loss dropped by more than 70%.
  • An older study found BHB cut leucine burning by about 30% and raised muscle protein synthesis by about 10%.
  • Bikman's takeaway: ketones are a fuel and also a signal to hold on to muscle.

Need to know: Directly relevant to keeping lean mass at a steady weight on a ketogenic plan. Research interest only.

Engineered probiotic 'living drug' senses glucose and makes GLP-1 in diabetic mice

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • Researchers took a probiotic bacterium long used in humans and added a glucose sensor (HexR) borrowed from another bacterium.
  • The switch makes the bacteria produce GLP-1 on demand when carbs show up.
  • Over 30 days in diabetic mice: lower fasting glucose and HbA1c, better insulin sensitivity, less fat gain with lean mass preserved, lower triglycerides and less inflammation.
  • Mouse data only so far.

Need to know: A creative twist on the GLP-1 story; a long way from people.

Bikman on carbs, insulin and testosterone: no essential carbohydrate, and insulin resistance is downstream of the receptor

For you 7 | Unique 5

  • There are essential fats and amino acids but no essential carbohydrate; liver and kidneys can make roughly 100-150 g of glucose a day by gluconeogenesis.
  • Insulin resistance isn't a receptor-binding failure: infusing insulin for 40 hours caused resistance with no change in binding.
  • Higher testosterone tracked with better insulin sensitivity, and weight lost best predicted testosterone gains across 24 studies.
  • He also notes that people regained about two-thirds of lost weight within a year of stopping semaglutide (STEP 1 extension).

Need to know: A good evidence summary for the 'why keto' side; his book The Carb Crisis is out Dec 15.
