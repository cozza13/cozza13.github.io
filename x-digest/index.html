Cancer updates

Glioblastoma

UCLA: T-cell density tracks long-term survival after pre-surgery dual checkpoint therapy in recurrent glioblastoma

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • Two UCLA Jonsson Cancer Center studies, senior author Robert Prins, released Oct 13.
  • Nature Communications study: nivolumab plus ipilimumab given before surgery drove more T-cell infiltration and interferon-related immune activity than nivolumab alone in recurrent glioblastoma.
  • Higher T-cell density in the removed tumor was an independent predictor of overall survival; long-term survivors had much higher tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes.
  • Second study: a new preclinical model (EGFRvIII, PTEN loss, CDKN2A loss) reproduces glioblastoma's immune-cold, checkpoint-resistant state, giving a tougher test bed for new immunotherapies.
  • Prins also co-authored a review on biomarker-guided immunotherapies for primary CNS tumors published Oct 10.

Need to know: The useful idea is a candidate biomarker: tumor T-cell density may help identify who responds to checkpoint combinations in glioblastoma. These are early, small-cohort findings, not a new standard. Source: UCLA Health release.

Other cancer

OncologyBench: AI agents often get every analysis right and still miss the final cancer decision

For you 7 | Unique 8

  • Latch released 25 multistep oncology evaluations built on 92 tasks, from basic research through diagnosis, treatment, resistance, and outcome.
  • 31 model-and-harness combinations were tested offline; Claude Opus 5, Opus 5.5, and Sonnet 5.5 led at 29 to 40% pass rates, with Kimi K3 at 24%.
  • Two-thirds of attempts that got every component analysis right still failed the final decision.
  • Answers also shifted depending on the persona of whoever asked the question.

Need to know: A sober data point for anyone using AI to reason over oncology data: specialist-level analysis doesn't yet add up to a reliable decision, so human review of the final call still matters.

For you 6 | Unique 6

Keto updates

RESET for Remission: 54% of young adults with type 2 diabetes reached remission in 24 weeks on diet plus supervised exercise

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Randomized 96 adults aged 18 to 45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes and obesity in England and Canada; published in The Lancet Regional Health, presented at EASD 2026.
  • Intervention: 800 to 900 kcal per day plus twice-weekly supervised aerobic and resistance training for 12 weeks, then maintenance.
  • Remission (A1c under 6.5% off medication for 12+ weeks) was 54% vs 4% with usual care.
  • MRI showed muscle volume preserved while intramuscular fat fell; caveats include an open-label design, 24 weeks, and no diet-only arm.

Need to know: Not a keto trial, but strong evidence that intensive dietary change reverses metabolic disease without new drugs, and that resistance training protects muscle during weight loss.
