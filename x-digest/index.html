Cancer updates

Seyfried lab separates the Crabtree and Warburg effects: cancer cells are less metabolically flexible

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • Dr. Duraj and colleagues in the Seyfried lab compared non-tumor and tumor cells.
  • "Dysregulated proliferation (i.e., cancer) is associated with lower metabolic flexibility and higher dependency on glucose and glutamine fermentation for sustained growth."
  • Seyfried says it "debunk[s] the myth" that normal proliferating cells show the Warburg effect.
  • Stated goal: "shifting attention from gene-linked to metabolism-linked therapeutic strategies."

Need to know: This is the scientific basis for metabolic approaches to cancer, including the glucose-and-glutamine targeting ideas behind ketogenic metabolic therapy research in glioblastoma. It's one lab's framing of its own paper, so look at the paper before leaning on it.

Universal germline testing: age cutoffs would miss most hereditary cancer variant carriers

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Cancer Discovery study of 39,184 patients across 32 solid tumor types: 16.3% carried a pathogenic germline variant.
  • Testing only patients diagnosed before 50 "would miss 72% of carriers"; 12.3% of late-onset patients still carried one.
  • @Aiims1742 pointed to a comparable NYU/Natera study of more than 153,000 people: strict criteria "may miss 22%" of actionable carriers.

Need to know: Two big datasets point the same way: test broadly, not by age or family history. The open question both posts raise is how often a found variant actually changes treatment.

Keto updates

Bikman: the 20 to 30 g protein-per-meal limit isn't real; 100 g kept muscle synthesis going for 12+ hours

For you 8 | Unique 6

  • The old cap "mostly came from studies where muscle protein synthesis leveled off after a modest dose of whey after lifting."
  • With 100 g of protein after exercise, "muscles kept making new protein for more than 12 hours", more than with 25 g, and "very little of the extra protein was burned off as fuel."
  • "Stop counting grams per meal and focus on the daily total."

Need to know: On a 3:1 ratio, protein is tightly budgeted. This suggests that how you split it across meals matters less than the daily total, which is useful when planning a 3:1 day. It's research framing, not a prescription.

Blood from 101-year-old donors turned into young-reading brain stem cells

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • Researchers in Bonn and Aachen used "just two genes" to convert blood cells directly into neural stem cells.
  • The cells read "about 13% as old as the donor" on an epigenetic clock, and about 5% when grown longer.
  • They kept dividing for up to six months and could mature into neurons and two kinds of brain support cell.

Need to know: This is a lab-dish result, but it means patient-specific brain cells could be made from a simple blood draw, even from very old donors. That's useful for modeling brain disease.
