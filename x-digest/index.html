Dr. Ken Berry on a one-year Virta study and the new NEJM CGM trial

For you 8 | Unique 5

  • Virta Health study: 262 adults with type 2 diabetes on very low-carb with coaching; average A1c went from 7.6% to 6.3%.
  • Insulin was cut or stopped in 94% of those on it, and sulfonylureas were stopped in all of them.
  • Usual-care comparison group of 87 had no significant changes.
  • He notes it was not randomized and Virta funded it; NEJM's CONNECT trial found a CGM helped people lower A1c.

Need to know: Supportive of low-carb for glucose control, with obvious limits: non-randomized, sponsor-funded. It fits your focus on keeping glucose low, but this is population data, not personal guidance.

Virta study · CGM trial

Muscle can take up glucose without insulin, so activity matters for insulin resistance

For you 6 | Unique 5

  • A thread explains that muscle contraction triggers glucose uptake through AMPK and calcium pathways even when insulin signaling is impaired.

  • The author emphasizes resistance training and a low-carb, protein-forward approach for patients with insulin resistance.

Need to know: A clear mechanism explanation that supports movement as a glucose lever alongside diet. It's an explainer, not new data.
