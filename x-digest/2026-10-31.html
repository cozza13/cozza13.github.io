The viral 'anti-diarrhea drug kills brain cancer' story is a 2021 lab-dish study

For you 7 | Unique 5

  • A post with more than 1,000 likes says loperamide (Imodium) can make aggressive brain tumor cells self-destruct.
  • The underlying work is a 2021 Goethe University Frankfurt paper in Autophagy showing loperamide and pimozide trigger ER stress and autophagic death in cultured glioblastoma cells.
  • It was done only in cells in a dish; loperamide is pumped out of the brain at normal doses, and high doses carry serious heart-rhythm risks.
  • Several replies flag exactly those limits.

Need to know: Interesting mechanism, old news, and nowhere near a treatment; worth recognizing when it gets forwarded to you.

Keto updates

Ben Bikman: bigger breakfast, lighter dinner, and why calorie math misses NEAT

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • In a 2013 study (Jakubowicz, Obesity), people with metabolic syndrome eating most calories at breakfast lost more weight and waist, and their insulin, glucose and triglycerides fell more than in the big-dinner group.
  • An egg breakfast kept people fuller than a bagel breakfast with the same calories, cutting lunch intake by about 160 calories.
  • He revisited a 1999 Mayo Clinic overfeeding study where fat gain varied tenfold, mostly explained by unconscious everyday movement (NEAT).
  • He also shared a study arguing low-carb diets improve overall cardiovascular risk markers beyond LDL.

Need to know: Meal timing and everyday movement both matter alongside carb restriction for insulin and glucose control.

Ketones and tissue protection: a review of ischemic injury research, plus beta-hydroxybutyrate and inflammation

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • A systematic review of mostly preclinical work found ketogenic interventions and beta-hydroxybutyrate often protected brain, heart and other tissues in models of stroke and heart attack.
  • Benefits seemed tied to a true ketogenic state, not just a high-fat, high-calorie diet.
  • A separate review summarized how beta-hydroxybutyrate regulates the NLRP3 inflammasome and ferroptosis pathways.
  • Both are framed as promising mechanisms rather than proven human effects.

Need to know: More mechanistic support for the idea that being in real ketosis, not just eating fat, is what matters.

The century-old history of the ketogenic diet for epilepsy

For you 6 | Unique 5

  • Russell Wilder at the Mayo Clinic designed the ketogenic diet in 1921 to mimic fasting's effect on seizures.
  • It faded with new drugs until Hollywood director Jim Abrahams revived interest in the 1990s for his son.
  • A neurologist reply notes the 2008 trial showed it works where drugs have failed (38% of children had seizures cut by more than half, versus 6%).

Need to know: A reminder that ketogenic therapy for the brain has a long clinical track record.
