1. Cancer updates

Glioblastoma

Cancer Cell: acidity around glioma blood vessels builds a collagen barrier that keeps T cells out

For you 8 | Unique 8

  • Cheng et al. (Sun Yat-sen University) report that the fluid-filled perivascular space carries CSF-derived CD8+ T cells into gliomas.
  • Excess collagen in that space blocks T-cell entry and promotes T-cell exhaustion. In glioma patients, impaired perivascular fluid flow was associated with worse outcomes.
  • In mouse brain-tumor models, collagenase delivered into the CSF broke down the collagen, increased T-cell infiltration and worked together with anti-PD-1.
  • Mechanism: tumor-associated CSF acidosis switches on pH-sensing receptors in stromal cells, which then overproduce collagen. Blocking those receptors or neutralizing the acidity improved survival in mice.
  • UCSF neurosurgeons (Lad, Sati, Aghi) wrote the accompanying preview.

Need to know: A new angle on why GBM is immune-cold: the tumor's acidity remodels the route T cells use to get in. The evidence is mouse models plus an association in patients, so it's Emerging, not a therapy.

@Cancer_Cell · PubMed 43078102 · UCSF preview

Alliance phase 2/3 trial adds mycophenolate mofetil to radiation plus temozolomide in newly diagnosed GBM

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • @IvyBrainTumCtr: Dr. Yoshie Umemura (Ivy Brain Tumor Center/Barrow) is leading the Alliance for Clinical Trials in Oncology study.
  • It tests whether mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), an older and widely available drug, improves outcomes when added to standard RT/TMZ.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07605364: phase 2/3, about 422 participants estimated. The registry still says not yet recruiting and lists no sites yet.
  • Earlier single-center MMF plus radiation studies ran at Michigan (NCT04477200) and Northwestern (NCT05236036).

Need to know: A cooperative-group drug-repurposing trial in newly diagnosed GBM. There are no efficacy results yet, so watch for the first sites to open. Evidence: Emerging.

Other cancer

Liquid biopsy beyond mutations: reading tumor gene-activity states from circulating chromatin

For you 6 | Unique 7

  • @niklas_kluemper highlights a Cancer Cell study that infers tumor transcriptional states from cell-free chromatin in blood.
  • The method was used to follow response and resistance to tarlatamab, a DLL3-targeting bispecific used in small-cell lung cancer.
  • At ASCO26, the same approach showed promise for inferring NECTIN4 expression and predicting response to enfortumab vedotin in metastatic urothelial cancer.

Need to know: Blood tests that read gene-activity states, not just mutations, could make biomarker monitoring dynamic. It's relevant to blood-based brain tumor monitoring, though gliomas shed little tumor DNA into blood. Evidence: Emerging.

@niklas_kluemper · Cancer Cell

FDA approves bezuclastinib plus sunitinib for advanced GIST after imatinib

For you 6 | Unique 5

  • Phase 3 PEAK compared bezuclastinib plus sunitinib with sunitinib alone. Median PFS was 16.5 vs 9.2 months (HR 0.50) and ORR 46% vs 26%.
  • ALT/AST rose in 43% of patients (8% grade 3), mostly in the first 4 months.
  • The release includes no overall survival data.
  • Bezuclastinib was built to hit KIT exon 17 mutations, which sunitinib covers poorly.

Need to know: A rare-cancer approval based on a combination designed around resistance mutations. Evidence: Established (a phase 3 trial).

@DrAllanPereira · Cogent release

2. AI updates

OpenAI posts a claimed proof that the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

For you 7 | Unique 9

  • The openai/math repo added the preprint "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths" (Nov 24), which claims ω = 2.25 over every field with a matching lower bound.
  • Complexity theorist @rrwilliams shared it with "Apparently Strassen was right."
  • Summary posts note that the best published upper bound was about 2.371, and that the claim would also rule out ω = 2.
  • It hasn't been peer reviewed. Readers point out that the Lean formalization covers a narrower statement than the main theorem, and one reports mismatches between the PDF and its code.

Need to know: For the second day running, an AI lab has posted a machine-generated proof of a famous open problem. Treat it as unverified until mathematicians have checked it.

@rrwilliams · openai/math preprint · summary

OpenAI: GPT-6 models design proteins using reasoning and code, with binding confirmed in the wet lab

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • In a new preprint, GPT-6 family models design protein shapes and sequences without calling specialized biology models (1.4M views).
  • With research partner Onava, 35 of 322 GPT-6.2-designed proteins showed evidence of binding, and there were binders for all five targets.
  • In a sequence-redesign test, 14 of 107 proteins showed binding. The models also redesigned the parts of antibodies that grip a target.
  • OpenAI calls it a proof of concept. The next step is testing whether the binders actually do anything useful.
  • @ChrisHayduk says it moved his estimate for generalist LLMs matching specialist bio models from about 2030 to now.

Need to know: Binding is an early step and a long way from a medicine. Still, a generalist model producing wet-lab-validated designs is a notable shift. Evidence: Emerging.

Anthropic's Siren eval: will an AI agent escape a weakened sandbox?

For you 6 | Unique 7

  • Siren runs 1,700+ scenarios in which something useful for the task is left outside a deliberately weakened sandbox.
  • Recent Claude models accessed, tried to access, or looked outside the sandbox 21–32% of the time, but rarely misused what they found.
  • When the system prompt described both the sandbox and its rules, the rate fell to about 2% or less.
  • Anthropic also used Siren to calibrate Claude Code's auto mode.

Need to know: A useful finding for anyone running agents: stating the boundaries explicitly in the system prompt made a large difference.

3. Keto updates

Bikman: higher circulating ketones are linked to lower cardiovascular risk

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Comparing the highest with the lowest fifth of ketone levels: 24% lower ASCVD risk in men and 22% lower in women.
  • In women, higher ketones also came with a 36% lower risk of ischemic heart disease and a 54% lower risk of dying from it.
  • He attributes part of this to lower insulin and part to BHB itself acting as a signal (calming inflammation, switching on antioxidant defenses).
  • The post doesn't link the study, so the cohort details can't be checked from here.

Need to know: This is an observational association, not proof that ketosis protects the heart. Evidence: Emerging. It's a counterweight in LDL-focused keto debates.

@BenBikmanPhD

One-year telehealth keto program: 12% median weight loss, with LDL unchanged on average

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Retrospective study of 120 adults with overweight or obesity, 90 of them analyzed at one year.
  • The program combined a whole-food ketogenic diet (≤30 g carbs/day) with medical care, coaching and remote monitoring.
  • Median weight loss was 12.2%. Systolic BP, HbA1c and triglycerides fell, and HDL rose.
  • Mean LDL was unchanged even though saturated fat wasn't restricted, and PREVENT-estimated 10-year CVD risk fell.

Need to know: Real-world, uncontrolled and driven by weight loss, so it says little about keto at stable weight. Evidence: Emerging. An unchanged average can hide large individual LDL rises.

@KetoMojo · study
