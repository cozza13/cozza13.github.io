Cancer updates

Glioblastoma

Duke's EGFR-targeted toxin D2C7 reports results in 81 recurrent glioma patients (Nature Medicine)

For you 9 | Unique 8

  • D2C7-ATC is an antibody-toxin aimed at EGFR that is infused directly into recurrent glioma.
  • The Duke team (Chandramohan, Desjardins) reported it was mostly well tolerated across 81 patients.
  • Median survival was 7.9 months in glioblastoma and about 17 months in certain biomarker subsets.
  • Published in Nature Medicine on Oct 16.

Need to know: One of the larger direct-infusion studies in recurrent glioma, and the biomarker split is the interesting part, since it hints at which tumors might respond. It's an early-phase result, so treat the survival numbers as signals, not comparisons.

Sancar lab tests temozolomide plus EdU to turn GBM drug resistance against the tumor

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • Nobel laureate Aziz Sancar and colleagues paired the standard GBM chemo temozolomide with EdU, a DNA-building-block analog.
  • In mouse models with U87 tumors, survival rose 130% versus placebo.
  • The post mentions a second model (GBM8) but is truncated there.
  • This is preclinical work only.

Need to know: Resistance to temozolomide is the central problem in GBM, so a strategy built specifically around it is worth tracking. It's mouse data, a long way from people.

New UK Lynch syndrome consensus: test for inherited risk upfront in dMMR/MSI tumors, including glioma

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • A new UK consensus on Lynch syndrome recommends upfront germline (inherited) genetic testing for tumors that are mismatch-repair deficient or MSI-high.
  • Glioma is explicitly included in the tumor types covered.
  • Lynch syndrome is an inherited condition that raises the risk of several cancers.

Need to know: A small but concrete change in how brain tumors with these markers get worked up in the UK, and it ties tumor biomarkers to family risk.

Nature study of 100,000+ people: brown fat linked to much lower diabetes and heart disease odds

For you 7 | Unique 6

  • Detectable brown fat was associated with 58% lower odds of diabetes and 47% lower odds of heart disease.
  • It also came with better metabolic markers and higher LDL cholesterol.
  • Nick Norwitz frames the higher LDL through his Lipid Energy Model rather than as a paradox.
  • In a second post he notes brown fat prevalence rises when outdoor temperatures fall, and suggests sleeping at 66 to 68F.

Need to know: Observational, so it shows association, not cause. The LDL angle is relevant to anyone on keto who sees LDL rise alongside otherwise good metabolic markers.

Bikman: weekend catch-up sleep doesn't undo the insulin hit from short weeknights

For you 7 | Unique 5

  • Young adults held to about 5 hours a night still gained weight, and insulin sensitivity dropped about 27% once short nights resumed after a weekend of recovery sleep.
  • One night of 4 hours' sleep lowered insulin sensitivity about 25% in healthy adults.
  • Four nights of 4.5 hours left fat cells needing nearly 3 times more insulin to respond.
  • Source study: Current Biology (2019).

Need to know: Sleep is a big lever on insulin and glucose, easy to overlook next to diet.

GLP-1 cautions: gallbladder disease doubles, and black-market retatrutide deaths

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • Gallbladder disease was more than twice as common with GLP-1 drugs as with placebo in weight-loss trials.
  • In a very-low-calorie diet study, 6 of 11 people eating 3 g of fat a day developed gallstones, versus 0 of 11 eating 12 g.
  • His explanation: dietary fat signals the gallbladder to empty, so very little fat lets bile sit.
  • Eric Topol reported two deaths linked to black-market retatrutide peptides, mostly sourced from China, and @agingroy noted a purity certificate doesn't prove sterility.

Need to know: Interesting mechanistic contrast with high-fat eating patterns like keto.
