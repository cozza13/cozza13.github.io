1. Cancer updates

Glioblastoma

Review: can oncolytic viruses exploit 9p21.3 (CDKN2A/MTAP) deletions in high-grade glioma?

For you 8 | Unique 8

  • MD Anderson neuro-oncology group (Fueyo, Gomez-Manzano et al.) in Neuro-Oncology Advances.
  • 9p21.3 is deleted in 20-50% of high-grade gliomas; the region holds CDKN2A/CDKN2B, MTAP and a cluster of type I interferon genes.
  • Losing CDKN2A/B removes a proliferation brake that viruses can exploit; losing MTAP and interferon genes weakens innate antiviral defense, so viruses may replicate better but immune priming may be weaker.
  • The authors argue 9p21.3 status could become a biomarker for virus therapy response, but say current preclinical models handle it poorly.

Need to know: A review, not new patient data (Speculation/Emerging). It is one of the few papers that treats the 9p21 deletion as an opportunity for immunotherapy rather than only a bad-prognosis marker.

PubMed 43066270 · DOI

GBM stem cells send NEAT1 RNA in exosomes to turn macrophages immunosuppressive (mouse study)

For you 6 | Unique 7

  • Posted by @exosome (Immortabio); paper in J Exp Clin Cancer Res.
  • Glioblastoma stem cells release exosomes carrying the long non-coding RNA NEAT1, which converts macrophages into immunosuppressive SIRPA+ cells.
  • Disrupting the pathway slowed tumor growth and extended survival in mice.

Need to know: Preclinical only. It adds to the evidence that GBM actively reprograms its immune neighbors, which is one reason checkpoint drugs have struggled in this disease.

@exosome · Paper

Neuro-Oncology Advances: phase 1 of a non-cooled laser ablation fiber, and PET changes beyond the tumor

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • Lund, Sweden: 14 patients (3 newly diagnosed GBM, 11 recurrent GBM or grade 4 astrocytoma, lesions under 10 cc) had MRI-guided laser ablation with an uncooled fiber.
  • All 14 completed; 7 of 22 adverse events were neurological, all mild or moderate; one procedure-related pulmonary embolism resolved; no device-related events.
  • Strasbourg: in 26 IDH-wildtype GBM patients, FDG PET showed lower metabolism in the "healthy" opposite hemisphere than in controls, so it may not be a clean reference for follow-up scans.

Need to know: Both are small and early. The laser study is about safety and workflow, not survival; the PET study suggests reading follow-up scans against the contralateral brain needs care.

3. Keto updates

Bikman: insulin resistance makes urine acidic, the main driver of uric acid kidney stones

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • Uric acid dissolves in near-neutral urine but crystallizes as urine gets more acidic, and those crystals can seed calcium oxalate stones.
  • Recurrent uric acid stone formers are severely insulin resistant; even in people without stones, lower insulin sensitivity tracks with more acidic urine all day, with normal uric acid output.
  • Mechanism: insulin signals the kidney to make ammonia, which buffers acid, and to raise citrate; kidney insulin resistance weakens both.

Need to know: Stones come up often as a ketogenic-diet concern. Bikman frames the bigger risk factor as insulin resistance, not uric acid alone. Mechanism plus observational data.

@BenBikmanPhD

Noakes questions the size of the benefit from lipid-lowering therapy: NNT 754 for all-cause death

For you 6 | Unique 6

  • @ProfTimNoakes quotes a meta-analysis: more potent lipid-lowering therapy cut all-cause death by about 8% (RR 0.92), an absolute risk difference that works out to treating 754 people to prevent one death.
  • He presents it as a challenge to LDL-focused prevention; it comes from the LDL-skeptic side of metabolic X.

Need to know: Relative vs absolute risk is the core of the LDL debate on keto. The quote is from one meta-analysis; the full paper and population matter. Opinion framing, not guidance.
