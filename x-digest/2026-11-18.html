1. Cancer updates

Glioblastoma

AIIMS pilot: Gamma Knife plus low-dose bevacizumab while patients wait for GBM surgery

For you 7 | Unique 8

  • AIIMS New Delhi neurosurgery (Deepak Agrawal) published a prospective pilot in Interdisciplinary Neurosurgery (November issue).
  • Context: waits from diagnosis to surgery there often exceed 3 months, so radiosurgery plus a single low dose of bevacizumab was offered before surgery.
  • 8 IDH-wildtype GBM patients; only 3 got it before surgery, 5 after. Median follow-up 12 months; 1-year survival 100%, 2-year 80%.
  • The authors call it preliminary and hypothesis-generating: tiny, no randomization, no control group.

Need to know: Interesting as a stop-gap idea for long surgical waits, not a new standard. Emerging evidence at best.

CYT-101: donor CMV-targeted T cells plus pembrolizumab in recurrent GBM (Phase 1 preprint)

For you 7 | Unique 7

  • medRxiv, Nov 17: 9 patients with recurrent GBM.
  • No dose-limiting toxicities, but no objective responses.
  • 6-month progression-free survival 11%, 6-month survival 55%, median overall survival 9.1 months.

Need to know: Safe but no efficacy signal yet. Not peer-reviewed. A useful counterweight to the more upbeat SNO immunotherapy headlines.

3. Keto updates

Skeptic thread gaining reach: "randomized keto plus fasting in recurrent brain cancer showed no survival benefit"

For you 8 | Unique 7

  • Yannick Buccella's post (about 5,900 views) argues against "starving" tumors: brains and hearts also light up on PET scans.
  • He cites a randomized ketogenic diet plus fasting trial in recurrent brain cancer with no survival improvement, a 7-patient fasting-before-immunotherapy study that only measured immune markers, and survivorship bias in positive studies.
  • Replies are split between keto advocates and skeptics.

Need to know: The strongest-reach keto-in-GBM conversation today, and a skeptical one. The randomized trial point is fair: human evidence for keto in GBM is still early, which is why trials matter.
